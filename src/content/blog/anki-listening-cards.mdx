I learn Mandarin with a teacher, and I keep an [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) deck of the characters we cover so they stick between lessons. Anki shows you a card just before you'd forget it, so the gap between reviews grows from a day to weeks to months for as long as you keep getting it right.

Each character has two cards. The Recognition card shows the character and asks what it means and how it sounds, so that's reading. The Listening card hides the written Chinese until you flip it, so you have to recall the character from memory, which is really practice for writing it.

I read, listen and speak Chinese with books, TV and my teacher, and I never need to handwrite it, so on 4 October I suspended every Listening card. Five weeks later I pulled the review log to see what that did.

Listening was half the deck and nearly three quarters of the time, and the share had crept up from 50% in February to 73% in September. Recognition didn't move, 8.2 to 8.6 minutes a day, so all 23 minutes a day is the saving.

## Why did Listening take so long?

It's a harder card. A Listening review took 17.5 seconds vs 8.8 for Recognition, and I got it wrong 17% of the time vs 11%. A wrong answer is the expensive part, because Anki drops the card back to short gaps and you see it again and again until it recovers. Over the next 90 days a wrong Listening answer cost me about 105 seconds of extra reviews, and a wrong Recognition answer about a minute.[^cost]

## Did my reading get worse?

Yes, and since the Listening card was a second look at every character it's not a surprise. In the five weeks before I suspended it I got about 8% of my reading reviews wrong, and in the five weeks since it's been about 15%.

That comparison is too kind to Listening though. I spent most of September in China reading signs and menus all day, so the weeks just before were my best in the log, and the cards coming up now aren't the same mix as then. Anki has a fairer yardstick built in. For every review its scheduler has a forecast of the chance I've forgotten the card, so I replayed the scheduler over my whole log and compared what it expected with what I did.

With Listening on I was at or under the forecast most weeks and never far over it. From May until I left for China I got 10.2% wrong where Anki expected 13.0%, because the scheduler treats the two cards of a character as strangers and never counted the Listening look. Since the switch-off I've got 14.6% wrong where it expected 12.3%. That's a swing of about 5 points (95% interval 2.6 to 7.6), and it's still growing, with the last week almost 9 points over.

For the first ten days nothing happened, which makes sense, because turning Listening off takes nothing away until a Listening review would have come up and didn't. Splitting on exactly that is the cleanest check I have. From 15 October, on characters whose Listening review I'd skipped I got 19.5% of my reading reviews wrong, and on characters whose Listening card wasn't due yet I got 3.7%. Anki expected about 10% for the first group and 9% for the second.[^skip]

The characters I've added since have never had a Listening card, and they're running 3 points over the forecast as well.

## Was it worth it?

The drop is real, but that only tells me it's there, not whether it matters. What matters is what I got for the time, so I priced both sides from my own review log.

A wrong reading answer costs me about a minute of extra reviewing over the next 90 days. The 5 points so far are about 2 extra wrong answers a day (I do about 40 reading reviews a day), and Listening took 23 minutes a day, so it was spending about 11 minutes to stop each one.

That's the average over five weeks in which a lot of cards hadn't missed a Listening review yet. If every reading review ends up like the ones that have, 12 points worse than with Listening, that's 5 wrong answers a day and Listening was paying about 5 minutes for each.

Even at its best that's about 5 times what a wrong answer costs, and tripling the cost to 3 minutes still doesn't get there. Listening would only have been worth it for my reading if a wrong answer cost me 5 minutes.

## What could I be getting wrong?

I didn't randomise this, so it's a before and after. The China trip sits right before the switch-off, which is why I left it out of the baseline, but coming home could have made my reading worse for a while on its own. The check on skipped reviews guards against that, because both groups came home on the same day, though they aren't the same characters.

Anki also re-fitted its scheduler on 9 September and has been giving me longer gaps since, which is another reason to compare against its forecast and not a plain before and after. I can't tell from the data whether I'm forgetting more or just pressing Again more readily, because Hard answers also went from 7% to 10% on the cards I'd started before September.

Five weeks isn't long enough to know where it settles. The last full week was my worst since March at 20% wrong, and Anki brings a card back sooner after a wrong answer, so some of the cost will turn up as extra reviews. So far reading takes the same time it did, 8.6 minutes a day.

I've also only counted what Listening did for my reading. If those cards were helping my listening, that isn't in any of these numbers, but I get that from TV and my teacher.

I'm keeping Listening off. Every number here comes from [this script](/blog/anki-listening-cards/measure.py), which reads the review log from Anki.

[^cost]: Review answers with 90 days of log after them, each compared with a right answer on a card at the same interval, then averaged over the wrong answers: 105 seconds for Listening and 61 for Recognition. Anki caps a review at 60 seconds and Listening hit the cap twice as often, so its time is if anything an undercount.
[^skip]: 452 reviews where the Listening card had come due and been skipped, 162 where it hadn't. The second group are older, better-known characters, which is why each is set against what Anki expected and not against the other. Once a Listening review was more than a week overdue the reading card ran at about 20% wrong.

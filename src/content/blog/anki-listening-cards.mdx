I learn Chinese with flashcards, and half of them were for handwriting. I turned those off and my
daily reviews went from 32 minutes to 9.

My reading did get a bit worse, but only enough to cost me about 2 minutes a day.

---

For the past 9 months, I have been learning Mandarin from the basics. Every week I have a 2-hour
in-class session with my teacher to practice speaking and reading. On top of that, I built a custom
Anki deck that was originally based off [Remembering Simplified
Hanzi](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/5862954-remembering-simplified-hanzi-1). However, I found
out that it wasn't very good for what I wanted to learn which was how to speak, read, and write
which is why I built my own.

If you don't know what [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) is, it's a tool to help with studying.
Basically flashcards, see [spaced repetition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaced_repetition). Now,
my deck had two different types:

<Flashcards hanzi="茶" pinyin="chá" meaning="tea" audio="/blog/anki-listening-cards/listen.mp3" />

- Reading -> practice the sound + meaning
- Writing -> practice handwriting + meaning

## Why did Writing take so long?

Writing was half the deck but 74% of my review time in the five weeks before I turned it off. An
average review took:

- 17.5s for Writing
- 8.8s for Reading

Fundamentally writing takes longer because I listen to the audio then write it down by hand. It's
much slower than the reading side which when you see the character, you either recognize it or not
in seconds.

## Danger, Here come the stats

To be scientific I'm giving each result with a confidence interval and a significance test, so each
figure will have the following.

- The result itself, like 35% of reviews
- The CI is a [confidence interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval), the range
  the true number plausibly sits in.
- The [p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-value) is the chance I'd see a gap this big if nothing had
  really changed

Typically in science you want to find the 95% CI, p < 0.05. There's more on [how each test
works](#tests) at the end.

## Did my reading get worse?

Yes, measurably so. I spent most of September in China reading signs and menus all day, so some of
the weeks just before were among my best in the log.

So 35% of my reviews in those five weeks I forgot or struggled on. The range is there because every
review has some luck in it. For example a coin flip: get 55 heads in 100 flips and that still
doesn’t make it a 55% coin.

Each line shows how far luck alone could have pushed that number up or down. With Writing it could
have been anywhere from 19% to 22%, and without it anywhere from 30% to 39%. Even my luckiest run
without Writing (30%) would still be worse than my unluckiest with it (22%), so luck can’t explain
the gap.

## How sure am I?

Sure that it isn't luck. If the Writing cards made no difference, luck alone would produce a gap
this big less than 1 time in 1,000. That rules out luck, not something else changing in October.

## Where did it get worse?

Two places:

- I forget characters I already knew a bit more
- New characters are harder to learn

- So on characters I already knew I went from 7% wrong to 11%.

The new ones do come later in the deck though, so some of that is them being harder characters.

These findings kinda match how it feels when learning, everything feels a bit fuzzier in my memory.
I no longer have the slow, methodical handwriting to solidify not just old characters but new ones
too.[^write]

## Was it worth it?

Hell no. Writing took 23 minutes a day but would only save about 2 minutes of reading reviews.

- I get about 2 extra reading cards wrong per day
- Each wrong reading card adds about a minute in extra review time over 90 days[^cost]

So dropping the Writing cards costs me an extra 2 minutes of reviews and saves 23, which leaves me
about 21 minutes a day better off.

## What's still open?

- **How big it ends up.** Five weeks is a short time. Anki also brings a card back sooner after a
  wrong answer, so some of the cost should turn up as extra reviews, though so far reading takes
  about the same, 8.6 minutes a day vs 8.2.
- **My handwriting.** That will fade, and I've decided I don't need it.
- **What the Writing cards did for my listening.** I've only counted reading inside Anki, and I get
  listening from videos and my teacher anyway.

## Closing remarks

I started with reading, writing and a bit of speaking because that's how Chinese school teaches it,
it's just the traditional way. But the reviews were taking longer and longer out of my day, and I
was dreading them and losing the motivation to do them at all.

That's why I even looked into it and it turns out the handwriting was costing me 23 minutes a day to
save 2 lmao.

For now I'm keeping the Writing cards off and I'll revisit the numbers here in a few months. Every
number here comes from [this script](/blog/anki-listening-cards/measure.py), which reads the review
log from Anki.

<details class="more" id="tests">
<summary>How each test works</summary>

- **Confidence intervals** are
  [bootstraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bootstrapping_(statistics)). I redraw the data with
  replacement thousands of times, work out the answer each time and take the middle 95%. Whole days
  or whole cards are redrawn together, because reviews on one day or of one card aren't independent.
- **Before vs after** uses a [permutation test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_test). I
  shuffle which days count as after 10,000 times and count how often the shuffled gap is as big as
  the real one. It almost never is, so p < 0.001. That's also where the saving at the top comes
  from: my total daily time fell 23 minutes (95% CI 17 to 29), with Reading flat at 8.2 → 8.6
  minutes.
- **The 22 earlier dates.** Reading bounces around from week to week anyway, so I ran the same 35
  days before vs after on every Sunday from 5 April to 30 August, when nothing had changed. The
  biggest jump at any of them was 2.3 percentage points. At the real date it was 7.5, or 5.8 if I
  leave the China weeks out of the before. The real date beat all of them, and 1 in 23 is p = 0.043.
- **Anki's own prediction.** In plain percentages my reading is 4.4 percentage points worse (95% CI
  1.9 to 6.9). Anki's scheduler also has a prediction of the chance I've forgotten each card, which
  I worked out by replaying it over my log. Against that it's 5.1 worse (95% CI 2.6 to 7.6).
- **Known and new characters.** A character is new for the 35 days after I first see it and known
  after that. New with Writing means first seen from 4 May to 6 August, and new without means first
  seen from 5 October.

</details>

[^cost]: Review answers with 90 days of log after them, each compared with a right answer on a card
    at a similar interval, then averaged over the wrong answers: 61 seconds for a Reading card and
    105 for a Writing card.
[^best]: The characters that have gone without a Writing review went from 14% wrong to 20%. If every
    review ends up like that it's about 2.3 wrong a day, so a little over 2 minutes. Against all my
    reviews from May to early September they're 9 percentage points worse (95% CI 6 to 13), which
    would be 3.8 minutes (2.4 to 5.2), but those are harder characters to begin with, so 4 is the
    top end.
[^write]: There's research on this too. [Reading depends on writing, in
    Chinese](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0503523102) (Tan and others, 2005) found that how well
    children read Chinese was closely tied to how well they could write the characters by hand.

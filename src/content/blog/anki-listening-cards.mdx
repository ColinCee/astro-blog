I learn Mandarin with a teacher, and I keep an [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) deck of the
characters we cover so they stick between lessons. Anki shows you a card just before you'd forget
it, so the gap between reviews grows from a day to weeks to months for as long as you keep getting
it right.

Each character has two cards. The Recognition card shows the character and asks what it means and
how it sounds, so that's reading. The Listening card hides the written Chinese until you flip it, so
you have to recall the character from memory, which is really practice for writing it.

I read, listen and speak Chinese with books, TV and my teacher, and I never need to handwrite it, so
on 4 October I suspended every Listening card. Five weeks later I pulled the review log to see what
that did.

Listening was half the deck and nearly three quarters of the time, and the share had crept up from
50% in February to 73% in September. Recognition didn't move, 8.2 to 8.6 minutes a day, so the
saving is 23 minutes a day (95% CI 17 to 29, p < 0.001).

## What do the brackets mean?

I'll give each result the way a paper would. The first number is my best estimate. The CI is a
confidence interval, the range the true number plausibly sits in, so a narrow one means I'm sure and
a wide one means I'm not. The p is the chance I'd see a gap this big if nothing had really changed,
and the usual bar is under 0.05, which is 1 in 20. There's a bit more on [how each test
works](#tests) at the end.

## Why did Listening take so long?

It's a harder card. A Listening review took 17.5 seconds vs 8.8 for Recognition, and I got it wrong
17% of the time vs 11%. A wrong answer is the expensive part, because Anki drops the card back to
short gaps and you see it again and again until it recovers. Over the next 90 days a wrong Listening
answer cost me about 105 seconds of extra reviews, and a wrong Recognition answer about a
minute.[^cost]

## Did my reading get worse?

Yes, and since the Listening card was a second look at every character it's not a surprise. In the
five weeks before I suspended it I got about 8% of my reading reviews wrong, and in the five weeks
since it's been about 15%.

That comparison is too kind to Listening though. I spent most of September in China reading signs
and menus all day, so the weeks just before were my best in the log, and the cards coming up now
aren't the same mix as then. Anki has a fairer yardstick built in. For every review its scheduler
has a forecast of the chance I've forgotten the card, so I replayed the scheduler over my whole log
and compared what it expected with what I did.

With Listening on I was at or under the forecast most weeks and never far over it. From May until I
left for China I got 10.2% wrong where Anki expected 13.0%, because the scheduler treats the two
cards of a character as strangers and never counted the Listening look. Since the switch-off I've
got 14.6% wrong where it expected 12.3%, so against the forecast my reading is 5.1 points worse (95%
CI 2.6 to 7.6, p < 0.001).

## How sure am I?

Reading bounces around from week to week anyway, so I ran the same before and after at 22 earlier
dates when nothing had changed. The biggest swing at any of them was 4.1 points, and at the real
date it was 7.5.

The cleanest check is inside the weeks since. Turning Listening off takes nothing away until a
Listening review would have come up and didn't, which is why the first ten days were fine. So from
15 October I split my reading reviews by whether that had happened yet. Where it had I got 19.5%
wrong and where it hadn't 3.7%, and Anki expected about 10% for both. That's 15.8 points apart (95%
CI 11.3 to 20.0, p < 0.001).[^skip]

The longer a Listening review had been skipped the worse it got, from 4% wrong when it wasn't due to
8% in the first week and about 20% after that (p < 0.001 for the trend), and then it levels off. The
five week average is lower only because cards join the skipped group one at a time, and by the last
week that was 73% of my reviews, so 12 points is roughly where it's heading. It also isn't me
pressing Again more readily, because on the cards that hadn't missed a review nothing moved.

## Was it worth it?

The drop is real, but that only tells me it's there, not whether it matters. A wrong reading answer
costs me about a minute of extra reviewing over the next 90 days (61 seconds, 95% CI 56 to 67). I do
about 40 reading reviews a day, so 5 points is 2 wrong answers a day and 12 points is 5.

So Listening's 23 minutes a day was saving me about 2 minutes of reading review, and about 5 at its
best. Even the top of what it saved (6.5 minutes) is nowhere near the bottom of what it took (18.8),
and it would only have paid for itself if a wrong answer cost me about 5 minutes and not 1.

## What's still open?

It's real and it wasn't worth it. What I don't know is exactly how big it ends up, because five
weeks is a short time however many reviews are in it. Anki also brings a card back sooner after a
wrong answer, so some of the cost should turn up as extra reviews, though so far reading takes the
same 8.6 minutes a day.

This is also one person and one deck, so it shows what happened to me and not what would happen to
you. And I've only counted what Listening did for my reading inside Anki. If those cards were
helping my listening that isn't in any of these numbers, but I get that from TV and my teacher.

I'm keeping Listening off, and I'll look again in three months. Every number here comes from [this
script](/blog/anki-listening-cards/measure.py), which reads the review log from Anki.

<details class="more" id="tests">
<summary>How each test works</summary>

- **Confidence intervals** are bootstraps. I redraw the data with replacement 10,000 times, work out
  the answer each time and take the middle 95%. Whole days or whole cards are redrawn together,
  because reviews on one day or of one card aren't independent.
- **Before vs after** uses a permutation test. I shuffle which days count as after 10,000 times and
  count how often the shuffled gap is as big as the real one. It never was, so p < 0.001.
- **The 22 earlier dates** are every Sunday from 5 April to 30 August. The real date beat all of
  them, and 1 in 23 is p = 0.043.
- **Skipped vs not due** is Fisher's exact test on 88 wrong of 452 vs 6 wrong of 162, p = 0.0000002.
- **The trend** across not due, 0 to 7 days, 8 to 21 days and 22 or more is a Cochran-Armitage test,
  z = 6.7.

</details>

[^cost]: Review answers with 90 days of log after them, each compared with a right answer on a card
    at the same interval, then averaged over the wrong answers: 105 seconds for Listening and 61 for
    Recognition. Anki caps a review at 60 seconds and Listening hit the cap twice as often, so its
    time is if anything an undercount.
[^skip]: The reviews where Listening wasn't due yet are older, better-known characters, which is why
    each group is set against what Anki expected. Against my baseline with Listening the skipped
    reviews are 12.3 points worse (95% CI 8.9 to 15.7).

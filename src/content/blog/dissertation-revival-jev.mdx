In [part 2](/blog/dissertation-revival-beating-2018/) my final agent needed 5M frames of practice to win 99% of Aliens games. Then TypeSafe released [Jev](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev) (I used version 1.14, from October 2026), a new kind of AI model built for quick decisions, so I wanted to see if it could play with no training at all.

It won 80% of games, about the same as just holding the shoot button, but it scored more points.

## What's Jev?

<Fig title="Each pass, and what it outputs">
  <Decide tokens={["The", "best", "move", "is", "left"]} choices={[{ name: "left", p: 42 }, { name: "right", p: 36 }, { name: "shoot", p: 22 }]} />
</Fig>

An LLM runs once for every word it writes, and then you have to dig the move out of its text. Jev runs once and gives every choice you hand it a probability, in about 200 ms.

Jev doesn't see the screen, so every move we write out where the ship, aliens, bombs and shields are as the `state`, and ask the same question with the same three choices. It sends back a probability for each (trimmed above to the parts we use), and we play the most likely one.

Every game Jev lost was a bomb landing on it. So we added a line telling it which bombs are within 2 columns of the ship and how far up they are, and it won 22 of the same 26, and 61 of the next 78 it hadn't seen.

Jev wins about as often as holding shoot.[^one] It scores more though, 48 points against 37, because it moves around and picks its targets, and its 130 games cost $0.83. My final agent is still way ahead, 99% and 83 points.

Each bar is the win rate over 5 sets of about 100 games, the bracket is their range, and Δ is the change from holding shoot, green when a significance test[^test] says it's real.

## Closing remarks

Even told where the bombs are, Jev still moved towards one in most of the games it lost, and it's rarely sure of a move, the one it picks averages 46%. I think it'd be great for the fuzzy decisions TypeSafe built it for, but in a game where one bad move ends it, practice still wins.

[^one]: Jev doesn't learn, so I played it on one set of 104 games to save credit, the same games my agents were tested on. Its points are well outside holding shoot's range on all 5 sets, its win rate isn't, and I picked the bomb line after looking at its first 26 games.
[^test]: [Welch's t-test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welch%27s_t-test) on the 5 sets: a gap counts as real when p < 0.05, as in [part 2](/blog/dissertation-revival-beating-2018/).

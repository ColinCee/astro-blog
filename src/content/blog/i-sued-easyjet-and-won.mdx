Last December easyJet moved our flight to Berlin a whole day earlier and
wouldn't put us on another airline, so my brother and I had to book KLM
ourselves for £544.92. Getting that back took eight months, a free complaints
service that sided with easyJet, and £127 to take them to court, where nobody
from easyJet turned up. In the end they paid £826.61, so we came out £154.69 up,
but we were out of pocket for 239 days first:

## Why was moving the flight a big deal?

An EU court has said a flight brought forward by more than an hour [counts as a
cancellation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62020CJ0146),
and ours moved by 20 hours. Once a flight's cancelled, [Article 8 of
UK261](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/261/article/8) says the airline
has to either refund you or get you there, and it's you that picks. We picked
getting there on the day we'd booked, which is why we asked for other airlines'
flights and, when easyJet didn't book any, booked KLM ourselves.

## Why did the free service say no?

ADR is a free complaints service, and for easyJet it's
[AviationADR](https://www.aviationadr.org.uk/), which says it's approved by the
CAA. They sided with easyJet because the change was made "more than 14 days
prior to departure". The 14-day rule is real, but it only switches off Article
7, the cash compensation, which we never asked for.

When I complained they said decisions are final, and didn't answer any of the
problems I'd pointed out with it. But an ADR decision only binds you if you
accept it, so we didn't, and took easyJet to court instead.

## What's it like taking an airline to court?

In Scotland it's [Simple
Procedure](https://www.scotcourts.gov.uk/taking-action/simple-procedure/civil-online).
You fill in a form online, pay a fee, £127 for us, and a sheriff hears the case,
no solicitor needed. England and Wales have the small claims track, which is
similar.

easyJet sent in a defence denying the claim, and to be honest it was a bit of a
mess:

It also never mentions the January emails. I still wrote up answers to the main
points, and we turned up to the hearing with a folder of printed emails. Nobody
from easyJet came, and the sheriff's order, the decree, just says "Alt: Absent".

The £826.61 is the £544.92 back, £27.59 of interest and £254.10 of expenses.
Only £127 of the expenses is the court fee, and the other £127.10 is for our own
time, which a court can award when you represent yourself. That and the interest
are the £154.69 we're up. easyJet kept what we'd paid for the original tickets,
since taking a refund would've stopped us claiming, but that's what the trip was
always going to cost.

Normally expenses on a claim this small are capped at £150, but [the cap doesn't
apply](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2014/18/section/81) when the other
side files a defence and then doesn't follow it through.

## Was it worth it?

Filing a defence costs an airline almost nothing, and turning up to court over
£544 doesn't make sense for them. I can't say whether that's deliberate, but the
effect is that plenty of people would give up the moment a defence lands. As an
hourly rate it definitely wasn't worth it, but it was very satisfying.

I've since sent the decision, the decree and the complaints service's reply to
the CAA and to CTSI, which I believe took over ADR oversight in April, so the
next person doesn't read "no entitlement is due under the Regulation" and
believe it.

In [part 1](/blog/dissertation-revival-speed/) Claude got my 2018 code training 65× faster. With runs that cheap, I wanted to see if today's methods could beat what I built as an undergrad.

In my dissertation my agent won 82.6% of Aliens games, but that was one run, counted over its last 500 training games while it still made 1 in 10 moves at random. Retrained five times on today's code and tested on 100 games each, it wins 73% and scores 47 points a game.[^2018] The best agent we got to won 495 of its 500 test games and scores 83. It even outscores a tree search that plans every move, and none of my learning agents had come close to that. All the code and results are in [my dissertation repo](https://github.com/ColinCee/GVGAI-Dissertation).

The first step is today's DQN with 5 times the practice, then each row adds one change to the row above, and the bars are win rates, since winning comes first and points only break ties. I only kept a change when a significance test over 5 runs said the gap was real, otherwise it's a tie and the simpler or cheaper option stays.[^test]

More practice did most of the work on wins, and the new reward took it to almost every game. After that, moving more often didn't change wins, but it added 20 points.

How to read the charts:

- **Each bar** is the average win rate of 5 runs, and the thin line across it is their range. My 2018 agent is striped, and the dashed line is what you win by just holding the shoot button.
- **Orange** numbers are real gains and **red** ones real drops, against the row they build on, and **grey** ones are ties. Win-rate changes are percentage points.
- **Bold** is what I kept for the next step.

## Do today's methods beat it with the same practice?

I tried three, each with the same 1M frames (2,000 games) my report's results came from:

- **[DQN](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14236):** DeepMind's 2015 Atari method. It scores each move from the screen and plays the best one. My agent is DQN plus 2 tricks.
- **[Rainbow](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02298):** DQN plus 6 tricks, including my 2.[^rainbow]
- **[PPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347):** a different kind of learner that picks moves directly.

## Step 2: which reward works best?

The reward is what the agent gets told is good, and so far it was just the game's points, one for every move that scores.[^clip] I tried:

- **Win:** +1 for a win, −1 for a loss.
- **Win + points ÷ 10:** the same, plus 0.1 for every move that scores.
- **My 2018 reward:** points and win, plus −0.5 on every move of a lost game.

## Step 4: how often should it move?

I did think about this one in 2018, I even wrote that holding each move for 4 frames, DeepMind's Atari setup, meant my agent could never reach some states, but at 12 hours a run I couldn't afford to test it. A tree search called MCTS picks a move every frame, and it scores 78 with no training at all.[^mcts]

Moving every 2 frames added 21 points, and moving every frame scored the same but took about 2 hours a run instead of 45 minutes, so every 2 frames stays.[^moves] Both beat MCTS's 78, and that's the end of the chain. It still loses about 1 game in 100, and the losses I could replay were all a bomb landing on the ship while it sat still and shot, which might be reaction time, but that's a follow-up.[^deaths]

## What did I learn?

- **Question the settings you copied.** Holding each move for 4 frames came from Atari, and here it cost about 20 points.
- **Check the dumbest baseline first.** Holding one button doesn't need any training, and I never tried it.
- **Run everything more than once.** My three 2018 runs of the same agent ended up to 14 percentage points apart on win rate.
- **Claude wrote all of the code, but it liked to stop early.** I pushed back six times in about 2½ hours.[^log]

[^test]: Welch's t-test over the 5 runs, each tested on 100 games: a gap counts when p < 0.05. Each step's footnote has its numbers, and the four steps I kept still pass a Holm correction across those four. [^rainbow]: Double DQN and prioritised replay (my 2018 agent's two), plus dueling networks, multi-step returns, distributional values and noisy networks. [The Rainbow paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02298)
[^runs]: DQN's five runs won 30% to 78%. [Results](https://github.com/ColinCee/GVGAI-Dissertation/blob/master/2026-revival/RESULTS.md)
[^button]: On the same 500 test games as today's agents: 82.4% wins and 37 points. PPO at 1M frames matched it game for game. My 2018 agent's code tests on other games from the same levels.
[^results]: On two of my other three games, one button wins every game: Deceptizelda (hold right) and Wafer Thin Mints (any direction). [Results](https://github.com/ColinCee/GVGAI-Dissertation/blob/master/2026-revival/RESULTS.md)
[^practice]: DQN, 1M to 5M frames: wins p = 0.013. DQN against Rainbow at 5M: wins p = 0.71, points p = 0.51.
[^reward]: Against points: win + points ÷ 10 wins p = 0.0015. Win against win + points ÷ 10: wins p = 0.19, points p < 0.001. I also tried points ÷ 5 and ÷ 20: ÷ 5 scored 1.6 more (p = 0.047), but picking the best of several options makes a lucky gap more likely, and a Holm correction across the two needs p under 0.025, so it's a tie.
[^network]: Wins p = 0.07, points p = 0.025.
[^moves]: Every 4 to every 2 frames: points p < 0.001. Every 2 against every frame: wins p = 0.19, points p = 0.24. Three of the five every-frame runs ran on an earlier version of the code with the same learning.
[^mcts]: One run of 51 games: MCTS doesn't learn, so its games only vary with the level.
[^clip]: As in DeepMind's Atari setup, the reward counts each scoring move as one, so a base and an alien count the same.
[^2018]: The report's Table 6.1 also gives 56.3 points, which I can't trace back to a log. My 2018 code tests on other games from the same levels as today's agents, and its 2,000 games come to about 1M frames.
[^deaths]: The 2 losses a replay brought back. Changing the points weight didn't clearly change how often it loses (3, 6 and 6 in 500 at ÷ 20, ÷ 10 and ÷ 5), and every frame lost none of its 500.
[^log]: From my Claude Code session log, 18 and 19 October 2026.

In [part 1](/blog/dissertation-revival-speed/) Claude got my 2018 code training 65× faster. With runs that cheap, I wanted to see if today's methods could beat what I built as an undergrad.

My 2018 agent wins 73% of Aliens games and scores 47 points a game. The best agent we got to wins 99 games out of 100 and scores 83. It even outscores a tree search that plans every move, and none of my learning agents had come close to that. All the code and results are in [my dissertation repo](https://github.com/ColinCee/GVGAI-Dissertation).

## How did we beat it?

Each row adds one change to the row above it, starting from today's DQN, which actually does worse than my agent at 1M frames, but it's what every later change builds on:

- **An extra 4M frames** of training, 5M in total.
- **Win + points ÷ 10**, a new reward: +1 for a win, −1 for a loss, plus a tenth of the points.
- **The IMPALA network**, a bigger one.
- **A move every 2 frames** instead of every 4.

Winning comes first, and points only break ties between agents that win. As far as I know, that's how the GVGAI competition ranks them too. So the bars are games won, and points sit next to them.

More practice did most of the work on wins, and the new reward took it to almost every game. After that, moving more often didn't change wins, but it added 20 points.

At each step I kept whatever won, wins first and then points, but only when a significance test over 5 runs says the gap is real, otherwise it's a tie and I keep the simpler or cheaper one.[^runs3]

Each chart below starts from what the step before kept and changes one thing:

- **Striped** is my 2018 agent, or my 2018 reward.
- **Grey** is where the step before got to, or the whole bar when a row dropped.
- **Orange** is what a change added, and **red** numbers are drops.
- **Bold** is the one I kept for the next step.
- **The dashed line** is what you win by just holding the shoot button.[^button] More on that below.

## Do newer methods beat it?

I tried three, each with the same 1M frames my agent got:

- **[DQN](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14236):** DeepMind's 2015 Atari method. It scores each move from the screen and plays the best one. My agent is DQN plus 2 tricks.
- **[Rainbow](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02298):** DQN plus 6 tricks, including my 2.[^rainbow]
- **[PPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347):** a different kind of learner that picks moves directly.

## What was PPO doing?

Holding the shoot button, and nothing else. That wins 82% of Aliens games, and my agent only won 73%, with its best run at 82%.

I never checked that in 2018. To be honest, Claude didn't think to at first either. Overnight, the second model it had checking its work spotted two PPO runs with exactly the same scores. Claude guessed they were just holding one button, so it checked.[^results]

My agent did learn something though. It gets 47 points a game against the button's 37, because it also shoots the bases in front of the aliens. So from here on:

- **Wins:** the button's 82% is the bar to beat.
- **Points:** show who actually learned to play.

## Which reward works best?

The reward is what the agent gets told is good. I tried mixes of three:

- **Points:** the game score.
- **Win:** +1 for a win, −1 for a loss.
- **2018 (mine):** win, plus −0.5 on every move of a lost game.

## How often should it move?

This is the one I never thought about in 2018. The agent holds each move for 4 frames, which is DeepMind's Atari setup. I suspected that makes some shots and positions impossible to reach. A tree search called MCTS picks a move every frame, and it scores 78 with no training at all.

Wins hardly move since they were already near the top, so points are where it shows:

- **Every 2 frames** scored 83 and won 495 of 500 games, in about 45 minutes a run.
- **Every frame** scored 82 and won all 500, but took about 2 hours a run.

That's a tie on both, so I kept every 2 frames since it's about 2½ times quicker to train, and that's the last row of the chart at the top. Both beat MCTS's 78 too, but every 2 frames does still lose about 1 game in 100.[^moves]

## Why does it still lose 1 game in 100?

I replayed the best agent's test games, and the 2 losses that came up again were the same thing: a bomb lands on the ship while it sits still and shoots. My guess was the reward, since each point is worth +0.1 and a death only costs −1, so taking a risk for more points is nearly free. But changing how much points are worth didn't clearly change it, points ÷ 20 lost 3 games in 500 and ÷ 10 and ÷ 5 lost 6 each.[^deaths]

Moving every frame with the bigger network lost none of its 500 though, so maybe it's just reaction time, but that's too few losses to tell, and a follow-up for another day.

## What did I learn?

- **Question the settings you copied.** Holding each move for 4 frames came from Atari, and here it cost about 20 points.
- **Check the dumbest baseline first.** Holding one button doesn't need any training, and I never tried it.
- **Run everything more than once.** My two 2018 runs of the same agent were 10 to 17% apart on games won.
- **Claude wrote all of the code, but it liked to stop early.** I pushed back six times in about 2½ hours.[^log]
- **I was the least informed person on my own dissertation.** About a third of my messages were me learning things like PPO and IMPALA.

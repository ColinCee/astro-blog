In [part 1](/blog/dissertation-revival-speed/) Claude got my 2018 code training 65× faster, so I wanted to see if today's methods could beat what I built as an undergrad.

My dissertation says my agent won 82.6% of Aliens games, but that was one run, measured while it was still training.[^2018] Retrained 5 times and tested properly, it wins 73% and scores 47 a game. The best agent we got to won 495 of its 500 test games and scores 83, which even beats a tree search that plans every move.

Each row adds one change, and I only kept it if a significance test over 5 runs said the gap was real, otherwise the simpler option stays.[^test] More practice did most of the work on wins, the new reward got it to almost every game, and moving more often added 21 points.

## Step 1: which method, and how much practice?

I tried three methods, first with the same 1M frames as my agent, then with 5 times that:

- **[DQN](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14236):** DeepMind's 2015 Atari method, and my agent is this plus 2 tricks.
- **[Rainbow](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02298):** DQN plus 6 tricks, including my 2.[^rainbow]
- **[PPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347):** a different kind of learner.

With the same 1M frames, DQN and Rainbow both do worse than my agent, and their runs vary a lot.[^runs] PPO looks like the winner since it wins the most, but it also scores the least, and it turns out it's just holding the shoot button, which wins 82% on its own, about what my dissertation said my agent won.[^button]

I never checked that in 2018, and to be honest Claude didn't either until its overnight reviewer spotted two PPO runs with the same scores.[^results] My agent did learn something though, it scores 47 against the button's 37 because it also shoots the bases, so the button's 82% is the bar for wins and my 47 is the bar for points.

With 5M frames, DQN and Rainbow get past my agent on both, while PPO stops just holding the button but doesn't win any more often. That's 17 minutes a run now, which would've been 2 or 3 days in 2018. DQN and Rainbow tie, so I carried on with the simpler, faster DQN.[^practice] I sorted out practice before the network because at 1M frames the runs varied too much to tell smaller changes apart.

## Step 2: which reward works best?

So far it's just been the game's points.[^clip] I tried:

- **Win only:** +1 for a win, −1 for a loss.
- **Win + points ÷ 10:** the same, plus a bit for scoring.
- **My 2018 reward:** points and win, plus −0.5 on every move of a lost game.

## Step 4: how often should it move?

I actually wrote in 2018 that holding each move for 4 frames meant my agent could never reach some states, but at 12 hours a run I couldn't afford to test it. A tree search called MCTS moves every frame and scores 78 with no training at all.[^mcts]

Every 2 frames added 21 points, and every frame scored the same but took 2 hours a run instead of 45 minutes, so every 2 frames it is.[^moves] It still loses about 1 game in 100, and the losses I replayed were all a bomb landing on it while it sat still and shot, which might just be reaction time.[^deaths]

## What did I learn?

- **Question the settings you copied.** Holding each move for 4 frames came from Atari and cost about 20 points here.
- **Check the dumbest baseline first.** Holding one button needs no training, and I never tried it.
- **Run everything more than once.** My three 2018 runs ended up to 14 points apart on win rate.
- **Claude wrote all the code, but liked to stop early.** I pushed back six times in about 2½ hours.[^log]

[^test]: Welch's t-test over the 5 runs, each tested on 100 games: a gap counts when p < 0.05. Each step's footnote has its numbers, and the four steps I kept still pass a Holm correction across those four. [Results](https://github.com/ColinCee/GVGAI-Dissertation/blob/master/2026-revival/RESULTS.md)
[^rainbow]: Double DQN and prioritised replay (my 2018 agent's two), plus dueling networks, multi-step returns, distributional values and noisy networks. [The Rainbow paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02298)
[^runs]: DQN's five runs won 30% to 78%. [Results](https://github.com/ColinCee/GVGAI-Dissertation/blob/master/2026-revival/RESULTS.md)
[^button]: On the same 500 test games as today's agents: 82.4% wins and 37 points. PPO at 1M frames matched it game for game.
[^results]: On two of my other three games, one button wins every game: Deceptizelda (hold right) and Wafer Thin Mints (any direction). [Results](https://github.com/ColinCee/GVGAI-Dissertation/blob/master/2026-revival/RESULTS.md)
[^practice]: DQN, 1M to 5M frames: wins p = 0.013. DQN against Rainbow at 5M: wins p = 0.71, points p = 0.51.
[^reward]: Against points: win + points ÷ 10 wins p = 0.0015. Win only against win + points ÷ 10: wins p = 0.19, points p < 0.001. I also tried points ÷ 5 and ÷ 20: ÷ 5 scored 1.6 more (p = 0.047), but picking the best of several options makes a lucky gap more likely, and a Holm correction across the two needs p under 0.025, so it's a tie. The ÷ 5 and ÷ 20 runs ran on later versions of the code with the same learning.
[^network]: Wins p = 0.07, points p = 0.025.
[^moves]: Every 4 to every 2 frames: points p < 0.001. Every 2 against every frame: wins p = 0.19, points p = 0.24. Three of the five every-frame runs ran on an earlier version of the code with the same learning.
[^mcts]: One run of 51 games: MCTS doesn't learn, so its games only vary with the level.
[^clip]: As in DeepMind's Atari setup, the reward counts each scoring move as one, so a base and an alien count the same.
[^2018]: The report says it played 50 test games, but 82.6% isn't a whole number of 50, and the log that gives it is the training one: games 1,500 to 2,000, while it still made 1 in 10 moves at random. The report's Table 6.1 also gives 56.3 points, which I can't trace back to a log. The retrained version is tested on 100 games per run, on other games from the same levels as today's agents, and its 2,000 games come to about 1M frames. All the code and results are in [my dissertation repo](https://github.com/ColinCee/GVGAI-Dissertation).
[^deaths]: The 2 losses a replay brought back. Changing the points weight didn't clearly change how often it loses (3, 6 and 6 in 500 at ÷ 20, ÷ 10 and ÷ 5), and every frame lost none of its 500.
[^log]: From my Claude Code session log, 18 and 19 October 2026.
